# Nurture and Show Rate: getting leads to book, show up and reach a close call

Open this sheet when leads opt in or book but do not show, when a team's calendar, contact speed, cadence or reminders need designing, or when a closer plans the hours between calls.
It holds the Four Pillars of Lead Nurture from the Lead Nurture playbook and Hunt Mode from the ACQ Closer Handbook (both 2025), scoped to medium-term nurture: the 30-day show rate, not long-term follow-up.
The call itself (Kill Mode: tone, discovery, offer, objections, looping) is sheet 09; referrals are sheet 14; the lead magnets and channels that produce the opt-in are sheets 04–06.

## The Four Pillars

#### 08.1. Four Pillars of Lead Nurture
- **Rule:** When show rates need raising, work all four drivers of show-ups, not one: Availability (the number of open appointment slots), Speed to contact (how fast you respond and how far out you let leads book), Personalization (communication useful and relevant to the lead) and Volume (how many times you reach out before giving up).
- Why it works: Reaching out the moment they express interest, making it convenient to talk, framing the conversation as beneficial to them and doing it as many times as you can gets more people to schedule and show up to buy, whatever you sell.
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: The pillars serve medium-term nurture, the time after a lead shows interest and before they buy, measured by the 30-day show rate; long-term follow-up (emails, podcasts, content) is a different job the author covers elsewhere.
- Anchor: «generate upwards of 20,000 leads a day), show-ups have four main drivers:» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Four Pillars of Lead Nurture

## Availability

#### 08.2. Availability tactics
- **Rule:** Raise availability with four tactics in this order: take appointments more days per week (all seven), more hours per day (a business selling nationwide in the US keeps at least 6 am to 6 pm PST open, 9 am to 9 pm EST in the checklist), more flexible start times (08.3), and inbound, outbound and self-scheduling options at the same time.
- Why it works: If leads do not schedule they cannot show, and if they cannot show they cannot buy: the businesses with the most time slots had the most schedules, shows and purchases, and the more ways people have to book, the more book; you pay rent, payroll and insurance seven days a week, and taking appointments on all seven makes a business 40% more available to accept money (2025).
- Bad → Good: Thank-you page saying you will be getting a text from this number shortly to book a time → the same plus a photo of Leila, who will reach out → text Leila at this number with the time that works for you to come in, which crushed: no one cared about faceless Alex, but once leads saw a real person both men and women were more likely to book (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar I: Availability, author note on the first online scheduler).
- When not to apply: The finding comes from appointment-based businesses, where a lead books a time and shows up for it; when the calendar fills with bad appointments, add friction instead (08.4).
- Anchor: «Take Appointments More Days Per Week: You pay your rent seven days per week.» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar I: Availability, Tactics

#### 08.3. Give Leads More Flexible Appointment Times
- **Rule:** Let leads pick a start time at least every 15 minutes, four options per hour, keeping the appointment length the same; do not offer starts only every 30 or 60 minutes because the appointment lasts 30 or 60 minutes.
- Why it works: Slots every 30 or 60 minutes make things convenient for the business, not for the lead; finer start times only widen the lead's freedom to choose when the appointment starts.
- Bad → Good: Scheduling only every 30 or 60 minutes because the appointments are 30 or 60 minutes long → four scheduling options per hour with the same appointment length.
- When not to apply: Done alone it produces awkward gaps between appointments and may take extra salespeople; for more expensive offers, qualify and close on separate calls, and a 15-minute qualifying call with separate calendars for setters and closers removes the gaps entirely.
- Anchor: «that makes things convenient for the business, not for the lead. Consider four scheduling» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar I: Availability, Tactics

#### 08.4. When to add friction instead of removing it
- **Rule:** Remove friction from booking by default, but when the calendar fills with too many bad appointments, add it back on purpose: a video or sales letter before the scheduler, a price on the page, a delayed scheduler.
- Why it works: Eliminating friction raises the number of appointments booked, the first step to a show; these steps lower the number of appointments and raise their quality.
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: Only when appointment quality, not quantity, is the constraint; while leads are not booking enough, keep removing friction (08.2).
- Anchor: «appointments on your calendar. When that happens—it’s okay to add friction.» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Author Note: Solution For Too Many Bad Appointments

## Speed

#### 08.5. Call leads within 5 minutes
- **Rule:** Contact every lead within five minutes of opting in, and treat speed as too slow if the team does not hear leads remark on how fast it was at least once a day (the Speed Litmus Test).
- Why it works: Conversions rise 391% when leads are contacted within the first 60 seconds (Velocify, cited 2025), and the faster you contact leads, the fewer times you have to contact them before they buy.
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: Reaching every lead immediately means carrying excess sales capacity; under 60 seconds is extra credit, not the bar.
- Anchor: «Call leads within 5min of opting in. Seriously, once you get this, you will see an» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar IV: Volume, 1) Scheduling Appointments

#### 08.6. Appointments inside 72 hours
- **Rule:** Set every appointment inside 72 hours (same day, next day or the day after) and do not let leads schedule more than three days out.
- Why it works: The shorter the delay between scheduling and the appointment, the higher the show rate; beyond three days more people schedule, but more people ghost.
- Bad → Good: Letting people book well beyond three days out → more schedules and more ghosts, a triple loss: the ghost itself, a sales team whose morale is killed by ghosted meetings, and a convenient slot stolen from someone who may have bought (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar II: Speed, speed to first appointment).
- When not to apply: You can get away with five days if you have to; keep tabs on show rates and find the sweet spot for your business, and when a meeting still sits more than three days out, fill the gap with value messages (08.22).
- Anchor: «When you speak to them, aim to set appointments inside 72 hours. So—same» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar II: Speed, Speed Summary

#### 08.7. Pull appointments forward
- **Rule:** When you call a lead who self-scheduled, make pulling the appointment forward the objective and end on one of five outcomes: no response → remove from the calendar (or double book if in person); unqualified → remove from the calendar and give free useful stuff, references or a fitting downsell; qualified and free right now → sell on this call; qualified → pull the appointment forward, same day if possible; qualified and cannot move → re-confirm it.
- Why it works: Show rates for same-day appointments are higher than for later ones, and the show rate for someone you are talking to right now is 100%.
- Bad → Good: Name yourself, say you are on a recorded line, name the booking, ask whether now is a terrible time; qualify who they are, what they want, what they are struggling with and what they have tried, and get them to agree it is a priority; then the good news that you can help plus a slot that just cancelled later the same day, "unless now is a good time?"; if the time cannot move, say more times are offered to cost them as little time as possible, restate the day and hour, and ask whether there is any reason they will not make it.
- When not to apply: Fresh leads come first: call self-scheduled leads to pull them forward in the gaps between appointments, once there are no fresh leads to contact and schedule.
- Anchor: «So that becomes the objective. When we call to schedule people, we have» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar II: Speed

#### 08.8. Hot handoff to the closer
- **Rule:** When the setter is not the closer, edify the closer and connect the lead with him in a three-way message while you still have the lead on the call.
- Why it works: Edifying the closer and introducing them in real time is the last step of working the lead for the maximum possible show-ups.
- Bad → Good: Tell the lead they are in luck, edify the closer by his expertise and by his results with people matching the lead's own description, check his calendar out loud, say whether the time moved or is confirmed, then promise a text introduction the moment the call ends and confirm the number to use.
- When not to apply: When the closer who will take the appointment is not available, hand off to an active closer instead.
- Anchor: «you connect them in a three-way message with the closer who is going» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar II: Speed, 2) Speed To First Appointment

#### 08.9. Time stamps are the first place to look
- **Rule:** When show rates drop, check the response time stamps on calls and messages first, rep by rep, especially when the drop belongs to one rep.
- Why it works: Leads make buying decisions before the sales call, so slow responses between scheduling and the appointment are where leads fall through the cracks.
- Bad → Good: A scheduled lead's question answered only on the next business day: the lead concludes you are scattered, disorganized and not prioritizing them, and stops asking.
- When not to apply: No special caveats in the source.
- Anchor: «So, if show rates drop, look here first—especially if they drop for a particular rep.» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar II: Speed, 3) Speed of Response

## Personalization

#### 08.10. Use The Lead's Preferred Communication Method
- **Rule:** Start the conversation with a new lead in as many ways as you can, in this order: call, and if unanswered call again immediately (double dial), then text, DM and email; continue in whichever channel the lead answers, and never text before you have double dialed.
- Why it works: Most communication attempts fail for reasons that have nothing to do with interest, a lead who opted in gave you permission to contact them, many phones block the first call from an unknown number and let the second through, and a live conversation beats a text thread.
- Bad → Good: Reaching out one time, on one platform → call, double dial, text, DM and email, then the channel the lead chose to answer in.
- When not to apply: Obey the laws of your area when double dialing.
- Anchor: «You will get higher initial response rates by starting conversations in as» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, 1) Use The Lead's Preferred Communication Method

#### 08.11. Local Area Code Call Wrapping
- **Rule:** Show an area code local to the lead on every outbound call, and send texts from devices whose messages read as coming from a real human rather than from an automated channel.
- Why it works: Local area codes have significantly higher pick-up rates than unrecognized or out-of-area codes, and messages that look sent by a real human get more responses than the ones spam and automated junk arrive as.
- Bad → Good: Texts arriving as green messages, the way spam and automated junk arrive → team iPhones sending blue messages, which get more responses (2025) (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar III: Personalization, pro tip on iPhones for texting).
- When not to apply: The tech will change over time; invest in whatever plays the role of pseudo-human verification, not in a particular device.
- Anchor: «Local area codes have significantly higher pick up rates than unrecognized or» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, Pro Tip: Local Area Code Call Wrapping

#### 08.12. Send The Best Leads To The Best Closers
- **Rule:** Route leads in five steps: study your best customers (the actions they take and who they are), ask those same questions in opt-ins, applications and the time before sales appointments, score each lead 1–5 or red-yellow-green by how qualified it is, route the best leads to the best closers, then collect the result.
- Why it works: Spread evenly, the best closer spends his time on unqualified people while the good leads go to junior reps; qualified before the call, the time goes to good leads and the bad ones are ignored.
- Bad → Good: A timeshare company's number one rep wasted two-thirds of his time on unqualified people while the good leads went to junior reps → given the best leads, he 5x'd the office's production and the company went from $200M to $1B a year in sales over the next five years (2025); a small weight loss company had every lead fill out an application before the sales call and cancelled the appointments of those who were not a fit, with exceptional show percentages and high profits (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar III: Personalization, qualify the leads).
- When not to apply: Routing is not licence for reps to drop the less qualified leads, which still close (08.30).
- Anchor: «Step 4 : route your best leads to your best closers.» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, 3) Send The Best Leads To The Best Closers

#### 08.13. Gift Card "Push" Incentive
- **Rule:** Before the appointment, send the lead a small gift they keep whether they show or not, any amount that is not insulting: a push incentive.
- Why it works: A push incentive is a social contract that hinges on reciprocity; it still works even when people know what you are doing, and it lifts close rate as well.
- Bad → Good: The day before, a text saying they should be caffeinated for the meeting, and a $5 gift card already in their email.
- When not to apply: Made conditional on showing up, it is no longer a gift; something the lead gets only after arriving is a pull incentive (08.14).
- Anchor: «they get the gift card you send whether they show or not. This is a» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, 5) Incentives

#### 08.14. A/B "Pull" Incentive
- **Rule:** Offer a scheduled lead a choice between two things they will receive at the appointment, both of which assume they show, and use it only when all three hold: the lead wants the thing, it connects obviously to what you will offer, and you incurred a real cost preparing it.
- Why it works: If the lead knows they will get value just for showing up, their risk drops and their opportunity cost of skipping rises; the choice binds because it shows you are incurring a cost on their behalf.
- Bad → Good: A beauty chain calls within five minutes of the opt-in and says free products are being set aside for a first-time client, face cream or hand lotion, and the pick will be waiting tomorrow; one ALAN beta tester, with the same messages and cadence as the rest of the test group, texted from a separate iPhone that a shirt was waiting, black or pink, and leads who named a colour showed up almost every time (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar III: Personalization).
- When not to apply: Missing any of the three conditions, the A/B offer stops being an incentive; do not confuse it with the push incentive (08.13): the first is a gift, this one is a bribe.
- Anchor: «A/B Incentives depend on three things. First, leads should want the thing. Second, it» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, 5) Incentives

#### 08.15. Where proof is woven in
- **Rule:** Send proof at three points: after the lead schedules and before they show, between the first and second call, and every 90 days to leads who ghosted, showing them the results of people who signed up around when they ghosted.
- Why it works: Seeing the results of people who signed up when they went quiet makes ghosted leads feel left behind while leaving them the option to make up for lost time.
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: No special caveats in the source.
- Anchor: «If they ghost, every 90 days. Show them the results of people who signed up about» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar III: Personalization, 6) Proof

## Volume

#### 08.16. The scheduling reach-out cadence
- **Rule:** Front-load reach-outs to a new lead over the first week: call within 5 minutes; double dial; leave a voicemail and text right after it; double dial and text two more times that day, a few hours apart; for the next two days call twice, once earlier and once later, texting after the second call; then call and text once a day for four days; after week one move the lead to long-term nurture; never give up before seven or more reach-outs in multiple ways.
- Why it works: The more days pass, the less likely the lead is to schedule, and most attempts fail for reasons that have nothing to do with interest, so the attempts are front-loaded and repeated.
- Bad → Good: Giving up after the first attempt, as nearly half of salespeople do (the average is 1.3 attempts, 2025) → the seven-day cadence, then long-term nurture of free value with soft calls to action, re-entering the cadence at the top once the lead re-engages (example — Lead Nurture playbook, Pillar IV: Volume, volume summary).
- When not to apply: Steps six and seven stay flexible, more days or fewer; as always, follow the laws in your area.
- Anchor: «1) Call leads within 5min of opting in. Seriously, once you get this, you will see an» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar IV: Volume, 1) Scheduling Appointments

#### 08.17. Automated reminder cadence
- **Rule:** Once an appointment is scheduled, send automated reminders immediately (confirming time, date, the number you will call from and the person they will meet), 24 hours before, 12 hours out and 3 hours out, and make it clear they are automated.
- Why it works: People don't mind reminders; they do mind being lied to; and stating the area code you will call from doubles pick-up rates (2025).
- Bad → Good: Automated reminders passed off as personal → reminders labelled as automated, each carrying the area code the call will come from.
- When not to apply: If you follow the speed pillar (08.5–08.7), you don't need to send that many anyway.
- Anchor: «Once you get the person scheduled, or they automatically schedule themselves, send» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar IV: Volume, 2) Reminding Them To Show

#### 08.18. BAMFAM: Book-A-Meeting-From-A-Meeting
- **Rule:** On every call that does not end in a close, book the next appointment on the calendar during that call, including the gap between a first and a second call; never get off with a promise to circle back later or to follow up with some times.
- Why it works: People are more likely to show up to appointments scheduled with them in real time, and once you put them in limbo they put you in their rearview mirror.
- Bad → Good: Ending with we'll circle back later → the next appointment set before hanging up, and a prospect who is dodgy about the next call handled as an objection.
- When not to apply: A call that ends in a close needs no BAMFAM; the closer's version, with the three reasons a call does not close, is 09.27, and the same move after the sale is 11.4.
- Anchor: «If you can’t schedule an appointment with them right then and» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Pillar IV: Volume, 3) Book Their Next Appointment At The Current Appointment

## Hunt Mode

#### 08.19. Work Your List: the three priorities
- **Rule:** Spend every hour that is not a close call in Hunt Mode (everything done to get prospects on the phone), working prospects in the order they are most likely to buy: 1) Inbound Sets, booked but not yet spoken to a closer; 2) BAMFAMs, who spoke to a closer, did not buy and have another close call booked; 3) Pipeline, who no-showed or declined and have not opted out, from today's back to 60 days and no further; begin texting only after the Inbound Sets are worked.
- Why it works: Hunting comes before killing because you cannot take a shot without something to shoot; the newest, freshest appointments have the highest value, so they are called first and the rest follow in descending likelihood of buying.
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: Anyone who opted out is off the list; the call itself is Kill Mode, everything done on the phone to get the sale, and belongs to sheet 09.
- Anchor: «You work the prospects in the order they are most likely to buy. To make it simple, we split them into three groups. Each is explained below.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), Hunt Mode #2 Work Your List

#### 08.20. Working Inbound Sets: The Full Process
- **Rule:** Take every Inbound Set to the close call by the fixed sequence: double dial to validate the contact (fake → cancel and opt out; picks up → pull the close call forward to right now, else to later that day); with no pick up, text Block 1 (their business), then Block 2 (Why Us, the pre-call video) and Block 3 (pull forward, slots open today), each only after a reply; with no reply to Block 1 send Non-Response 1 after 12 hours and Non-Response 2 after 24 hours, and cancel 12 hours before the call; when Block 2 or 3 goes unanswered or the call is not pulled forward, send the night-before and morning-of texts.
- Why it works: The process makes sure none of these precious prospects slip through the cracks: pulling the call forward turns the freshest appointment into a close before the prospect cools, and the cancel frees the slot before the call time is wasted.
- Bad → Good: Non-Response 2 does not chase with another reminder but says someone else is requesting the time and asks for a quick bell emoji to hold the spot, since a request that costs one tap gets an answer where a question does not; with silence 12 hours out, the cancel text excuses it as busy business-owner life and offers tomorrow in the same message, so the lead is not lost with the slot (example — ACQ Closer Handbook, Inbound Set Text Script).
- When not to apply: Only between the booking and the close call; a lead who picks up but has no time now gets 30 seconds of qualifying questions (type of business, rough revenue, what interests them) and a check that they saw the video, instead of the pull-forward.
- Anchor: «This process breaks down taking an Inbound Set all the way to a Close call. You need to know this very well.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), Hunt Mode, Working Inbound Sets: The Full Process

#### 08.21. Every booked appointment is manually nurtured
- **Rule:** Nurture every booked appointment by hand between the set and the call, from a real cell phone and on top of the automated reminders (08.17): an intro text with the pre-call video, a night-before message, a morning-of message and one about 60 minutes before.
- Why it works: Manual nurture has boosted show rates more than anything the author has seen across his portfolio (2025).
- Bad → Good: The setter starts a group chat and sends the video, saying it will save time on the call tomorrow; the closer says hello right after and maintains the chat from then on; with no reply by end of day he asks whether they saw the video; the morning-of text carries a testimonial link, and an hour before comes a short "Talk soon."
- When not to apply: No special caveats in the source.
- Anchor: «Manual nurture has boosted show rates more than anything I have seen across our portfolio.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ Outbound Reminder Text Script, Why this is important

#### 08.22. More than three days out means value messages
- **Rule:** When a BAMFAM is booked more than three days out, fill the gap with a value-based message (a testimonial, a third-party video review or a value video from YouTube) on top of the texts five minutes after the call, the night before and the morning of.
- Why it works: A long gap is filled with proof rather than silence, and the five-minute text leaves a positive feeling after the conversation.
- Bad → Good: The three texts of a booked follow-up: five minutes after the call, so great talking with you, excited to continue the conversation and potentially do business together, we can help; the night before, looking forward to our meeting tomorrow; the morning of, talk today; and when the meeting is more than three days out, one of a testimonial, a third-party video review or a value video from YouTube (example — ACQ Closer Handbook, ACQ BAMFAM Script).
- When not to apply: This is the fallback; the target is a meeting inside 72 hours (08.6).
- Anchor: «Send a value-based message with one of the following:» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ BAMFAM Script, IF BOOKED MORE THAN 3-DAYS

#### 08.23. A text back is answered with a call
- **Rule:** Use texts to get a phone call: when a prospect texts back, call immediately and text again only if they do not pick up; when an exchange reaches one or two replies and a typing bubble, call; answer an objection raised over text by setting an appointment, and push any question that needs a long answer to the call.
- Why it works: The point of a text is a conversation: on the phone you can close, pull forward or confirm them, and if they cannot talk the thread simply continues, so go for the phone call whenever you can.
- Bad → Good: Typing out paragraphs to overcome an objection in the thread → a text that sets an appointment.
- When not to apply: Short questions over text are answered as soon as possible; only long answers move to the call.
- Anchor: «When they text back, call them immediately.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), Hunt Mode, Texting Guidelines

#### 08.24. No-show cadence
- **Rule:** When a prospect does not pick up at the scheduled time, double dial, then text at once; if nothing comes back within five minutes, text again with two reschedule times for tomorrow; if there is still no response, add them to the end-of-day outbound block, and if they pick up there, run the Outbound Set Phone Script.
- Why it works: The missed slot is recovered the same day rather than written off, and a pickup drops straight back into the set script.
- Bad → Good: "Just tried your cell. Ready to rock?"; five minutes of silence; then business-owner life is busy, happy to find another time, two openings tomorrow, either of those work?
- When not to apply: No special caveats in the source.
- Anchor: «Add these to your outbound block at the end of the day if you do not get responses. If they pick up → OUTBOUND SET PHONE SCRIPT» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ No Show Script

#### 08.25. ACQ Pipeline Phone & Text Script
- **Rule:** Work a stalled or ghosting prospect by calling first about the obstacle holding the deal and, if they do not answer, texting in escalating steps (a five-minute follow-up right after the unclosed call, value messages, check-ins, bumps, a re-offer), until the deal closes or a date to touch base again is agreed.
- Why it works: Each step gives the prospect a new reason to reply rather than repeating the ask, and the five-minute text leaves a positive feeling after the conversation.
- Bad → Good: After the call, a thank-you saying you can help; then a case study of a company like theirs that solved their specific problem; a question on how they feel about the decision; a bump asking whether to assume it is not a priority; a re-offer of a few newly opened spots before they are announced.
- When not to apply: For a deal waiting on something off the call with no BAMFAM secured, or a prospect who has ghosted; a booked follow-up gets the BAMFAM texts instead (08.22).
- Anchor: «Start by calling. If they do not answer, transition to text. The goal is to close the deal or agree on a timeline to touch base again.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ Pipeline Phone & Text Script

#### 08.26. The two outbound priorities and Pickup Primetime
- **Rule:** Give every hour left after working your list to setting your own appointments, never less than two hours: at Pickup Primetime, the end of the day, drop everything for two hours of outbound; spend outbound time on referrals first (see sheet 14) and the remainder double dialing and texting unscheduled leads who opted into the marketing list, newest to oldest.
- Why it works: Pick-up rates are highest at the end of the day, which maximizes the return on that time; closers who do more outbound get more deals, since referrals should net an extra deal a day and opt-in calling should produce about two sets an hour, four sets yielding at least one close (2025).
- Bad → Good: No example in the source.
- When not to apply: A live call is the one thing Pickup Primetime does not interrupt.
- Anchor: «You create your own opportunities by making outbound calls to set your own appointments. First, you will do outbound after working your list. Second, you will do outbound during Pickup Primetime.» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), Hunt Mode #3 Outbound

#### 08.27. Cash thresholds that keep a set alive
- **Rule:** When "too expensive" comes up on a setting call, first ask whether it is budget or value; on value, continue the script; on budget, ask permission for financial questions (monthly cash flow, then cash on hand) and set the consultant call when they have $15K or more in cash or $10K a month of cash flow (2025).
- Why it works: Splitting the objection turns an unanswerable "too expensive" into either a value conversation the script already handles or a factual question about money with a threshold answer.
- Bad → Good: The prospect says it is too expensive; the rep asks whether it is more a budget thing or whether, seeing the value, he would consider it; on budget he asks about monthly cash flow and cash on hand, and at the threshold says there might be a way to make it work and asks to set the consultant call.
- When not to apply: Below the floor the call does not proceed to a set: the rep would not be calling if the prospect did not meet the minimum revenue mark, and the financial questions confirm that a deal is possible at all.
- Anchor: «IF THEY HAVE $15K+ CASH OR $10K/MO CASH FLOW» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ Outbound Set Phone Script, Obstacles to Book a Meeting

#### 08.28. Play out both scenarios
- **Rule:** When a prospect on a setting call gives a reason to wait (a business problem to solve first, a solution already in place, a busy season, no time to leave the business), do not contradict it: ask permission to offer a perspective, show that each branch of their own reasoning ends with the meeting being worth having, then ask again.
- Why it works: Both branches of the prospect's reasoning are made to end in the same action, which removes the reason to wait without contradicting him; treated as the very problem the product addresses, the objection strengthens the ask.
- Bad → Good: "I need to solve a business problem first" → if you solve it, we are booked out at least six weeks anyway, so the timing works and we tackle the next set of problems, and if not, the team helps break past it, a win-win closed with "would it be crazy just to have a conversation about it?"; "I can't leave my business" → normalise it among other attendees, name it key man risk, say the product exists to save time by solving the problem faster, and reframe the trade as reallocating time rather than taking it away.
- When not to apply: When the argument is the cost of waiting (a busy season), keep the disclaimer inside the pitch: no promises or guarantees.
- Anchor: «Got it. So you solve that and we get you out… and get you out here…I think we are booked out at least 6 weeks anyways?…so it actually works well…(haha) then we can tackle the next set of problems…» — ACQ Closer Handbook (2025), ACQ Outbound Set Phone Script

## Execution

#### 08.29. Track and rank three metrics per rep
- **Rule:** Track and rank show rate, close rate and lead-to-close ratio for every sales rep, never close rate alone, and pay on them, with a commission bump tied to show rate in the neighbourhood of 5–30% of what the rep makes on a sale (2025).
- Why it works: The show-rate list measures work ethic and the closing list measures skill, and the only thing that actually matters is the conversion rate on the leads you already have; culture is the rules that govern good and bad behaviour, so the behaviours that get leads worked are paid for as well as praised.
- Bad → Good: A business obsessed with close rate by rep → three ranked lists; a rep with a very high close rate who does not work their leads is seen as entitled and wasteful, and is made aware of it.
- When not to apply: No special caveats in the source.
- Anchor: «Track and rank: show rate, close rates, and lead-to-close ratio by sales rep.» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Lead Nurture Checklist, Execution

#### 08.30. Cherry-picking leads: "Yellows are the new gold"
- **Rule:** Do not let reps take only the prime leads and hand off or drop the less qualified ones: with leads scored red (truly unqualified), yellow (less qualified) and green (prime), the yellows get worked.
- Why it works: Less qualified leads still close, and an under-qualified lead is not wasted time but free practice at the script and the objections; some people who look unpromising turn out to be the opposite.
- Bad → Good: The other reps cherry-picked and gave their yellows to Jacob, a new salesman, who worked them in off-hours and on weekends, kept closing them, earned the nickname "garbage man" and finished as the top salesman out of 26.
- When not to apply: Do not confuse the colour score with a different system: it is the same scoring, in another form, as the 1–5 used to route the best leads to the best closers (08.12).
- Anchor: «They just wanted to cherry-pick. So they gave him all their “garbage” leads. And he would» — $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (2025), Execution
